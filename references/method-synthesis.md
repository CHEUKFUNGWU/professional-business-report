# Method synthesis

This skill combines methods extracted from the two user-supplied report corpora. It is an independent synthesis and does not imply endorsement by PwC, Strategy&, Goldman Sachs, or their affiliates.

## What the PwC-derived method contributes

- decision framing and issue trees;
- mixed-method research using data, surveys, interviews, cases, and expert analysis;
- value-chain, opportunity, maturity, capability, and operating-model analysis;
- movement from findings to business implications and implementation;
- executive summaries, assertion-led sections, and practical recommendations;
- close attention to samples, definitions, notes, proxies, and caveats.

## What the Goldman Sachs-derived method contributes

- clear, falsifiable calls with a defined horizon;
- comparison with consensus, plans, guidance, pricing, or a dominant baseline;
- driver decomposition and reconciled contribution bridges;
- distinction between temporary and structural forces;
- forecast review, revision, and error attribution;
- base, upside, and downside scenarios with catalysts;
- sensitivity, invalidation conditions, and indicators to monitor;
- distinction between a correct fundamental view and a decision-relevant gap.

## How the combined method works

The PwC-derived side answers:

`What is happening, why does it matter to the organisation, and what should it do?`

The Goldman Sachs-derived side adds:

`Compared with what, driven by which variables, under which scenario, and what would prove the view wrong?`

The combined report therefore follows:

`question -> baseline -> evidence -> drivers -> finding -> scenario -> implication -> choice -> action -> monitoring`

## What was deliberately excluded

- imitation of either firm's branding, report layout, tone, or proprietary labels;
- claims of access to private analysts, clients, models, or datasets;
- automatic investment recommendations in ordinary business reports;
- mandatory forecasts when the evidence or assignment does not require them;
- generic transformation advice detached from findings;
- decorative acronyms, artificial numbered frameworks, and inflated consulting language;
- regulatory boilerplate unrelated to the actual author, audience, and jurisdiction.

## Design principle

The report type controls the method. The method does not control the report type. A course data-analysis report prioritises reproducibility and rubric compliance. A board strategy paper prioritises choices and trade-offs. A market outlook prioritises forecast discipline. All three share evidence traceability and clear reasoning.
